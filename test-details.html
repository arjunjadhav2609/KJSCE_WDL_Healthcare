Laboratory Test Details

CBC Test

Purpose

A Complete Blood Count is a common blood test used to check overall health, screen for medical issues, find the cause of unexplained symptoms like tiredness or fever, and monitor treatments or chronic diseases

Sample Required

A Complete Blood Count test requires a small venous blood sample typically 2 to 3 milliliters
Drawn from a vein in your arm, usually inside the elbow. The blood is collected directly into a lavender or purple-top vacuum tube containing EDTA, an anticoagulant that stops the blood from clotting

Preparation Requirements

No special preparation or fasting is usually needed for a Complete Blood Count (CBC) test. Eat normally: Food does not change basic blood cell counts. Drink water: Drink plenty of water before the test. Good hydration makes your veins larger and easier to find. Avoid alcohol and smoking

Normal Reference Range

RBC Count: 4.5 - 5.5 million/µL
WBC Count: 4,000 - 11,000/µL
Platelet Count: 150,000 - 450,000/µL

Reference ranges may vary slightly depending on the laboratory and equipment used.

Report Delivery Time

Reports are typically available within 6 to 8 hours of sample collection.
